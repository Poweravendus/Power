### Summary of the preliminary view

- **What it is.** A tender-driven energy services contractor: onshore seismic (shrinking, 5% of the order book), oilfield O&M and integrated field development for Vedanta, coal handling plant EPC for Coal India subsidiaries through unincorporated JVs, and from Sep-2025 a Gulf crew-supply business (Kuiper) that is 38% of revenue at a 7% margin. A 50% participating interest in one small oil field; five more blocks arrive with the Oilmax merger.
- **The numbers.** FY26 revenue Rs.791cr (+70%, of which Rs.299cr is Kuiper's seven months; organic +6%), EBITDA margin 12.1% (25.5% in FY22), PAT Rs.51.8cr, ROE 11.5%, three-year operating cash flow minus Rs.24cr against Rs.119cr of PAT, receivables plus unbilled at 209 days, gross debt Rs.158cr from Rs.24cr. Incremental pre-tax return on the Rs.406cr of capital added since FY20: 8.6%.
- **The pattern.** Five cycles in 34 listed years, each carried by one or two contracts and ended when they did; the current cycle depends on Vedanta (about 40% of the book) and MCL (about 26%). Twelve of 27 public promises since FY20 were missed; FY26 standalone grew 6% against a 40-50% guide.
- **The merger.** 117 AESL shares per 10 Oilmax shares, set in Sep-2025 on FY25 earnings of Rs.48.6cr; Oilmax reported a Rs.12.2cr loss for the nine months to Dec-2025. The share count rises from 4.86cr to about 8.80cr and public holders' claim on AESL's own earnings falls from about 44% to about 24%; Oilmax must earn about Rs.42cr a year for the swap to be neutral to them. Promoter holding falls to 49.45%.
- **The price.** About 40x FY26 EPS and 21x EV/EBITDA, on about Rs.1cr a day of BSE turnover with no institutional holder. The price discounts the order book, the FY27 guide and E&P optionality before any of them has appeared in a reported quarter.
- **Preliminary view.** Cautious. A decent CHP and Vedanta franchise inside a poor capital-allocation record and an expensive, about-to-dilute stock. The falsification test and the five questions for the model are in R20.

## R1. Explain it like I'm new to this industry

**The one-sentence version.** Asian Energy Services (AESL) is a contractor. It does not own oil wells or coal mines in any size that matters (yet); it gets paid by the companies that do, for doing specialised, labour-and-equipment-heavy jobs they would rather not do themselves. The customer is almost always a large oil company (ONGC, Oil India, Vedanta/Cairn, Hardy, Sun Petrochemicals), a Coal India subsidiary (Eastern Coalfields, Central Coalfields, Mahanadi Coalfields), a state utility (Assam Gas Company, Gujarat State Electricity Corporation), or, after the 2025 Kuiper acquisition, international oil majors and drilling contractors in the Middle East and South-East Asia. (AR FY26, p.22-23, Board's Report; Order-Wins folder; AR FY26, p.218-219, Note 45 segment note.)

The company reports two segments: "Oil and Gas" (seismic, O&M, field development, Kuiper manpower) and "Mineral and other energy sectors" (coal handling plants and material handling). FY26A consolidated revenue was Rs.791.0 crore, of which Rs.632.7 crore was Oil & Gas and Rs.158.4 crore Minerals [A] (AR FY26, p.218, Note 45). Of the Rs.791.0 crore, Rs.299.4 crore came from outside India — that is Kuiper, consolidated for seven months [A] (AR FY26, p.219, Note 45 III).

Below is what each line of business actually is, who pays, and how the money is earned.

### 1. Seismic data acquisition (the original business, since 1998)

**What it is.** Before anyone drills an oil well they need a picture of the rock layers underground. Seismic surveying creates that picture by sending sound energy into the ground and recording the echoes. A **2D** survey records along single lines (a cross-section); a **3D** survey records across a grid (a volume). The data is then processed and interpreted by geoscientists to decide where to drill.

**What a crew does.** A seismic crew is a few hundred people with trucks, cables, thousands of small sensors (geophones) and an energy source. Historically the energy source in India was dynamite in shallow "shot holes" (AESL's first operation in 1997 was shot-hole drilling, AR FY21, p.3 "Our Journey"). The modern alternative is **vibroseis**: large trucks with a vibrating plate that shake the ground on a programmed sweep. AESL commissioned its first vibroseis units in mid-2020 for a Rajasthan project (Press Release 13-Jul-2020). The crew surveys the line, obtains land access and forest/environmental permissions, lays out sensors, "shoots" each point, picks up and moves on. It is a mobile, seasonal business (no work in the monsoon), and it is **asset-light in the sense that the fleet is small, but working-capital-heavy** because the crew is paid monthly while the customer pays on certified progress.

**Who pays and how.** The customer is the exploration company that holds the block: ONGC and Oil India (state-owned, the bulk of Indian acreage), GAIL, and private players such as Sun Petrochemicals. Contracts are won in tenders. Pricing is on units of output: the company's own revenue-recognition policy says seismic revenue is "measured based on milestones reached, units delivered, efforts expended, number of shot points/kilometers covered, etc. as per the terms of contract" [A] (AR FY26, p.85, Note 2(b)). In practice this means a rate per **line-km** (2D) or per **square km / shot point** (3D), with mobilisation and demobilisation paid separately. The specific unit rates in AESL's contracts are **not disclosed**. Examples of order sizes: ONGC 3D Gujarat Rs.78.24 crore over 11 months (Order 19-Jun-2023); GAIL Rajasthan Rs.16.69 crore over 6 months (Order 2-Aug-2023); Oil India 2D Rajasthan ~Rs.82 crore (Press Release 29-Sep-2024); Sun Petrochemicals 3D Gujarat ~Rs.46 crore over 12 months (Press Release 16-Jul-2025) [M].

**Why the industry exists this way.** Exploration companies have a lumpy need for seismic crews (a block gets surveyed once, then not again for years), so they rent crews rather than keep them. Government policy — the HELP/OALP/DSF licensing rounds (glossary) — determines how many new blocks need surveying, so demand follows policy cycles. Seismic was only ~5.5% of AESL's order book in mid-2025 [M] (Press Release 1QFY26), which tells you management has deliberately shrunk its dependence on it; the 9-Mar-2024 press release describes a deliberate move "to diversify from cyclical business which also frequently involves force majeure situations, to stable business with long term contracts".

### 2. Oilfield O&M — running somebody else's production facility

**What it is.** Once a field is producing, the oil company needs people to run the plant 24x7: operate separators, compressors and pumps, maintain equipment, manage safety, handle crude and gas. **O&M (operations and maintenance)** contracts outsource this. AESL's reference contract is for Vedanta (Cairn Oil & Gas) at **Suvali**, Cambay basin, Gujarat: operating Vedanta's onshore oil-and-gas processing facility and offshore platforms, awarded 24-Jan-2022, Rs.248 crore including GST for four years with a one-year extension option, work started April 2022; the extension was exercised in March 2026 to run to 31-Mar-2027 "on the same rates, terms and conditions" [M] (Order 24-Jan-2022; Order 16-Mar-2026). Earlier, AESL's first O&M contract (2017-18) was for a floating production unit (FPU) at the EBOK field offshore Nigeria, and in 2023 it won an FPSO O&M letter of award in the PY-3 field (AR FY26, p.3 "Our Journey"; Order 21-Mar-2023). An **FPSO** is a ship that produces, stores and offloads oil at an offshore field.

**How it is priced.** Typically a fixed monthly fee for a defined manpower-and-services scope, sometimes with reimbursable spares, over a multi-year term. AESL's contracts' actual fee structure is **not disclosed**; the Vedanta number above implies roughly Rs.5 crore a month before GST, but that is our arithmetic on the headline, not a disclosed rate.

**The step-up: integrated field development.** In July 2025 Vedanta awarded AESL a ~Rs.772 crore (ex-GST; ~Rs.865 crore with GST) "integrated service contract" to be executed over 57 months [M] (Press Release 1QFY26; Transcript 2QFY26). This is bigger than O&M: AESL takes on drilling new wells, workovers and construction of production facilities as a single turnkey package for the field owner. Management describes this as the model it wants to replicate (Transcript 4QFY26). Sister contracts of the same type were pre-approved with the parent for Oilmax's Tiphuk (Rs.75 crore) and Duarmara (Rs.150 crore) blocks [A] (AR FY26, p.34, AOC-2).

### 3. Coal handling plants (CHP) — EPC and BOOT for Coal India subsidiaries

**What a CHP is.** At an opencast coal mine, raw coal has to be crushed, sized, stockpiled and loaded onto rail wagons quickly ("rapid loading", often through a **silo**). A coal handling plant is the conveyor-crusher-silo system that does this; "first-mile connectivity" in Coal India's language. Coal India wants to move coal by rail rather than road, so its subsidiaries have tendered many CHPs since 2020.

**How AESL earns.** Two models:
- **EPC (engineering, procurement, construction):** AESL designs, buys the equipment, erects and commissions the plant for a lump sum, usually with O&M during a defect-liability period (DLP). Example: Mahanadi Coalfields, Lakhanpur, Odisha — ~Rs.459 crore including GST, seven years including O&M, won with a JV partner, the largest CHP order in AESL's history [M] (Press Release 4-Nov-2025). Central Coalfields Rs.250 crore (Press Release 6-Feb-2024); Singareni coal-evacuation conveyors Rs.148.5 crore + GST over 18 months with JV partner Indwell (Press Release 18-Jun-2024); first order outside Coal India: GSECL Ukai thermal station CHP enhancement, Rs.187.62 crore incl. GST, 2-3 years (Press Release 22-Jun-2026).
- **BOOT (build-own-operate-transfer):** AESL funds and builds the asset, owns and operates it for a term while the customer pays a tariff, then hands it over. Example: Eastern Coalfields Hura CHP (3 Mtpa), Rs.128 crore incl. GST — build, then operate and maintain for five years (Order 14-Feb-2022); Assam Gas Company compressor station on BOOT for three years, ~Rs.200 crore of which ~Rs.82 crore in year one (Press Release 17-Dec-2024).

**Mechanics that matter for the numbers.** CHP work is done through **unincorporated joint ventures** with partners who bring pre-qualification (Zuberi, FFIL, Indwell, Global); AESL books "sale of services" to the JV and the JV bills the customer, so a large part of standalone receivables are due from these JVs (R15). Revenue is recognised by percentage of completion, which the new auditor flagged as the key audit matter [A] (AR FY26, p.68-69). Minerals revenue fell from Rs.272.6 crore in FY25 to Rs.158.4 crore in FY26 because projects were between phases and the monsoon delayed execution [A/M] (AR FY26, p.10, p.218).

### 4. International manpower and O&M — Kuiper (from September 2025)

Kuiper Group (UAE/Cayman, bought from private-equity owner Gulf Capital for USD 9.25 million cash, completed 29-31 Aug 2025) is "a global provider of diverse, fully integrated manpower solutions to the energy industry" [M] (Order 18-Apr-2025; Press Release 18-Apr-2025). Plainly: it recruits, employs, visas, houses and deploys skilled oilfield workers (operators, technicians, drilling crews) to oil companies, drilling contractors and EPCI yards in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, UAE, Oman, Egypt, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Brunei, Singapore, Namibia and Cyprus, mostly under long-tenure master service agreements. Revenue was USD 82 mn (CY22), 74 mn (CY23), 68 mn (CY24) [M] (Order 18-Apr-2025 annexure). Economics: gross margin ~14-15%, EBITDA ~7%, net ~6% because of low-tax jurisdictions [M] (Transcript 2QFY26); run-rate Rs.40-45 crore a month [M]. It is a volume, low-margin, people business; it carries little capital but a lot of working capital (Kuiper came with Rs.240.6 crore of current assets against Rs.73.0 crore of current liabilities at acquisition [A], AR FY26, p.228, Note 55).

### 5. Owning a slice of an oil field — the Indrora participating interest

In 2023 AESL bought a 50% **participating interest (PI)** in the Indrora field, Gandhinagar, Gujarat, from its parent Oilmax (Rs.15 crore upfront plus "progressive cash flows linked consideration" [A], AR FY23, p.27 AOC-2; Rs.17.7 crore recorded as acquisition of PI in FY24 [A], AR FY24, Note 38). A PI is a share of the costs and the crude of a block under a revenue-sharing or mining-lease arrangement with the government; AESL's share of proved reserves was 1.328 million barrels at 31-Mar-2026 (up from 0.811 mn after a 0.527 mn addition) with production of 0.010 mn barrels in the year [A] (AR FY26, p.231, Note 57). The Mevad NM-01 well, drilled in January 2026, is producing ~100 bopd on test [M] (Press Release 31-Jan-2026). The company accounts for its share of the Indrora joint operation line by line and reimburses Oilmax (the operator) for its share of costs and for government's share of revenue [A] (AR FY26, p.135-139, Note 44). This is the thin end of a wedge: after the Oilmax merger (R2), field ownership becomes a third reporting segment [M] (Transcript 4QFY26).

### Where the money really comes from

Three customers were each more than 10% of FY26 revenue; the top customer was Rs.143.0 crore (18% of revenue) and the top three Rs.264.4 crore (33%) [A] (AR FY26, p.219, Note 45 IV). The payers are overwhelmingly Indian public-sector oil companies, Coal India subsidiaries and one large private E&P company (Vedanta), plus, via Kuiper, Gulf national oil companies and their contractors. Government capex policy (OALP/DSF rounds, Coal India's rail-loading push, Assam gas-grid connectivity) drives the pipeline; AESL's margin comes from executing on time with a mostly hired fleet and a 434-person permanent workforce on the parent's rolls [A] (AR FY26, p.40, Annexure D) and 500+ in the group [M] (AR FY26, p.2).

### Glossary (terms used in this note)

- **2D / 3D seismic** — subsurface imaging by recorded sound echoes; 2D along lines, 3D over a grid.
- **Vibroseis** — truck-mounted vibrator used as the seismic energy source instead of explosives.
- **Line-km / shot point** — units in which seismic work is measured and billed (kilometres of 2D line; individual energy-source points).
- **O&M** — operations and maintenance of a production facility under a multi-year service contract.
- **FPSO / FPU** — floating production, storage and offloading vessel / floating production unit used at offshore fields.
- **EPC** — engineering, procurement and construction: a turnkey build for a fixed price.
- **BOOT** — build-own-operate-transfer: contractor funds and owns the asset, operates it for a fee over a term, then transfers it to the customer.
- **CHP** — coal handling plant (crushing, conveying, silo and rapid wagon loading at a mine).
- **DLP** — defect liability period after commissioning, during which the contractor maintains the plant.
- **PI** — participating interest: a percentage share of the costs and production of an oil/gas block.
- **DSF / OALP / HELP** — Discovered Small Field bid rounds; Open Acreage Licensing Policy; Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy — the government's schemes for awarding blocks.
- **RSC** — revenue sharing contract between the government and a block holder.
- **CBM** — coal-bed methane, natural gas produced from coal seams.
- **BOPD / BOEPD** — barrels of oil (equivalent) per day.
- **LoA / LoI** — letter of award / letter of intent from a customer; the LoA is the firm order.
- **L1** — lowest bidder in a tender (likely to be awarded).
- **JV (unincorporated)** — a contractual joint venture that bids and bills in its own name; AESL consolidates its share.
- **NCLT** — National Company Law Tribunal, the court that sanctions mergers.
- **ESOP** — employee stock option plan.
- **Warrants (preferential)** — instruments sold to selected investors at a fixed price, 25% paid upfront, convertible into shares within 18 months on paying the balance 75%.
- **AOC-1 / AOC-2** — statutory annexures listing subsidiaries/JVs and material related-party contracts.
- **KMP / SMP** — key managerial personnel (MD, CFO, CS) / senior management personnel.
- **ID / NED** — independent director / non-executive director.
- **CARO** — Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, the auditor's compliance checklist.
- **RPT** — related-party transaction.
- **ICD** — inter-corporate deposit (a loan between companies).
- **NCI** — non-controlling interest (minority shareholders' share of a subsidiary).
- **SOFR / MCLR** — floating-rate benchmarks for USD and INR bank loans.
- **ADGM / DMCC** — Abu Dhabi Global Market and Dubai Multi Commodities Centre free zones where group companies are registered.

## R2. Company overview and history

### What it does today, for whom, where

AESL is a Mumbai-headquartered (3B, Omkar Esquare, Sion East) services company with three engines [M] (AR FY26, p.11 "Strategy"):

1. **Indian energy services** (the listed parent, standalone revenue Rs.491.8 crore FY26A [A], AR FY26, p.22): seismic crews for ONGC/Oil India/GAIL/private E&Ps; O&M and integrated field development for Vedanta (Suvali and the Rs.772 crore integrated contract), Hardy (PY-3 subsea flowlines, LoI ~Rs.48 crore, Press Release 14-Mar-2024) and the Amguri field; CHP/material handling for Coal India subsidiaries, Singareni, Assam Gas and GSECL. Standalone order book ~Rs.1,750 crore at 31-Mar-2026 (68% oil & gas, 32% minerals) [M] (AR FY26, p.18); Rs.1,754 crore at 30-Jun-2026 [M] (Press Release 1QFY27). Operating locations are project sites (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Assam, Jharkhand, Odisha, Telangana, Tamil Nadu offshore); the company states it has "no plant" [A] (AR FY26, p.62). The vibroseis fleet size and crew count are **not disclosed** in FY26 documents.
2. **Kuiper Group** (international manpower/O&M, consolidated from 1-Sep-2025): ~USD 50-65 mn annual revenue, ~8% EBITDA, target USD 100 mn by FY30 [M] (Transcript AGM Sep-2026; AR FY26, p.11).
3. **Upstream ownership**: 50% PI in Indrora (Gujarat) today; after the Oilmax merger, PIs in Amguri, Duarmara, Tiphuk (Assam), South Rewa CBM (Chhattisgarh), a Kutch offshore DSF-IV block and a quartzite mine (Uttarakhand) [M] (Sep-2025 merger deck, slide 13; Order 1-Sep-2026).

### Origin and promoter history

- **Incorporated 9-Mar-1992** as Asian Oilfield Services Ltd, Mumbai; IPO in 1995; first seismic contract 1998; 2D contracts for Premier Oil (2003) and in the North-East (2007) [M] (AR FY21, p.3; Merger outcome 6-Sep-2025, Annexure A).
- **Samara Capital era (2008-2016).** Samara Capital Partners Fund I (Mauritius), with Global Coal and Mining Pvt Ltd as person acting in concert, became promoter in 2008 and held 56.32% [A] (AR FY17, shareholding section). The Samara years included a Kurdistan (Iraq) branch run through the Dubai subsidiary DMCC, Samara ICDs of Rs.17.6 crore and Rs.34.2 crore to the group, and a pledge of 2.2 million shares by Samara [A] (AR FY17, financial notes). The Iraq business ended badly: the group "no longer has any presence" in Iraq, wrote off Rs.2.71 crore of Kurdistan equipment in FY17 [A] (AR FY18, consolidated PPE note) and DMCC pursued a USD 2 million London arbitration against a customer, later stayed for settlement talks [A] (AR FY20, p.22-23, auditor's emphasis of matter reproduced in Board's Report).
- **Oilmax takeover (FY17).** Oilmax Energy Pvt Ltd (OEPL; promoted by Kapil Garg and family, incorporated 2-Aug-2008) "took over the management of the Company" on 5-Aug-2016 and by 16-Nov-2016 had acquired Samara's 12,572,600 shares (56.32%) under a share purchase agreement; promoter reclassification was approved by shareholders on 28-Sep-2016 and BSE on 16-Nov-2016 [A] (AR FY17, Note 43 "Change in ownership"; shareholding notes). The purchase price is **not disclosed** in the AR extracts. Oilmax was then allotted 14.5 million warrants on 27-Dec-2016, converted in tranches (2.5 mn Feb-2017; 3.4 mn Sep-2017; 4.1 mn Mar-2018), and a non-resident investor took preferential shares (1.25 mn Mar-2017; 4.5 mn Mar-2018), taking paid-up shares from 22.3 mn to 38.07 mn [A] (AR FY17/FY18 shareholding notes; AR FY20, p.23). Warrant price not found.
- **Rename (2020).** Shareholders approved an object change on 11-Sep-2020 to add coal, minerals and other energy services; the name became **Asian Energy Services Ltd** by fresh certificate dated 1-Oct-2020 [A] (AR FY21, Board's Report).
- **Oilmax merger (2025-26).** Oilmax, the 60.83% parent, is being merged into AESL: board approval 6-Sep-2025; swap 117 AESL shares per 10 OEPL shares (Bansi S. Mehta valuation: OEPL Rs.4,349.03/share on DCF only; AESL Rs.371.96/share, the ICDR floor, versus a relative value of Rs.357.20) [S/M] (Merger Valuation Report, p.10-11); BSE/NSE no-objection letters 2 and 5 Mar-2026; NCLT Mumbai order 22-Apr-2026 convening meetings; shareholders approved 11/12-Jun-2026; petition admitted 7-Jul-2026; **NCLT sanction pronounced 29-Sep-2026** (CP(CAA) 95/MB/2026); certified copy received 15-Oct-2026 [A/M] (AR FY26, p.25; Merger 30-Sep-2026 and 15-Oct-2026 filings). Appointed date is the first day of the month in which the effective date falls (NCLT order para 8). Post-merger, promoter holding falls from 60.97% to 51.71% (54.81% to 47.30% fully diluted) and share count rises to ~9.2 crore [M] (Sep-2025 merger deck, slide 'Shareholding Pattern'; Merger Pre & Post SHP file). Oilmax's 68 public shareholders (34.78% of OEPL) become direct AESL shareholders [A] (Merger Pre & Post SHP, OEPL Table I).

## R4. Business cycles: what the revenue really was

Headline point: in 34 years as a listed company AESL has never had a revenue base that survived the loss of one contract or one customer. Each of the five cycles below was carried by one or two awards; each ended when those awards ended, were suspended or were disputed. The segment notes, the major-customer disclosures and the MD&A sentences prove this, cycle by cycle.

### Cycle 1 – pre-FY14: small domestic seismic contractor under Samara Capital

- Revenue driver: 2D/3D onshore seismic for ONGC and small operators, shot-hole drilling. Order book Rs.114cr (Jun-2008) (AR FY08, p.109) [M]; Rs.80cr (AR FY10, p.280) [M]; Rs.28cr at 31-Mar-2012 (AR FY12, p.441) [M]. FY13 revenue Rs.56.0cr (AR FY14, p.17, five-year table: Rs.559.96 million) [A].
- Cost structure: crew-heavy, equipment-light; the Dec-2013 deck states "all under performing and under utilised assets are being retired. Proceeds will be used to retire debt" (PPT Dec2013, p.8) [M].
- What happened: Samara took a controlling stake (2008–10 per the Dec-2013 timeline) and in 2013 installed an ex-Schlumberger/WesternGeco team under CEO Rahul Talwar (PPT Dec2013, p.7) [M]. The market view was a turnaround story on the back of "Western Geco's exit from land seismic" (PPT Dec2013, p.6) [M].
- Lesson: the Indian onshore seismic market was and is tender-driven and intermittent ("In India, the activity continued to be low and sporadic", AR FY14, p.17) [M]. Nothing in the record suggests the domestic base alone could sustain a Rs.100cr+ company.

### Cycle 2 – FY14–FY16: Kurdistan

- Revenue driver: one "mega contract" in Iraqi Kurdistan (Shakal, Gazprom Neft), executed Jul–Nov 2013, followed by a Gazprom 2D vibroseis job (completed Q3 CY2013) and a >500 sq km 3D award from Oil Search Iraq in Q4 CY2013 (PPT Dec2013, p.4; AR FY14, p.17) [M]. The company called it "our first large 3D job in Kurdistan in Northern Iraq… Deployment of Asian Oilfield's largest ever 3D crew… First ever Vibroseis operation… First ever Crew outside of the US to deploy in excess of 4000 real time wireless remote units" (AR FY14, p.17) [M].
- Numbers: revenue Rs.125.3cr FY14 (from Rs.56.0cr FY13) (AR FY14, p.17) [A]; Rs.147.4cr FY15 (AR FY15, p.26, total revenue Rs.14,738 lakh) [A]; Rs.77.7cr FY16 (AR FY16, p.20, Rs.7,766.76 lakh) [A]. Net loss FY16 Rs.27.1cr (AR FY16, p.20) [A]; net loss FY17 Rs.18.4cr (AR FY17, p.19) [A]. Gross block FY14 Rs.108cr (model) [A].
- Cost structure: the Kurdistan spread was bought for the job ("Significant investments have been made to induct best in class equipment on an exclusive basis", PPT Dec2013, p.8) [M]; funded partly by a 15.25–16% inter-corporate deposit of Rs.11.5–25cr from Global Coal & Mining (AR FY14, p.34; AR FY16, p.74) [A].
- What the market believed: "Path to USD 75 mm+ revenues in near term", "Scope for at least 4 number of Crews in the Middle East, Africa and Asia", "Pre-qualifications with World's leading Oil and Gas Companies such as Gazprom" (PPT Dec2013, p.19, p.6) [M]. Peak revenue reached was Rs.147cr (c. USD 24m) in FY15.
- What actually happened: oil collapsed and Kurdistan became a war zone — the FY15 AR already speaks of executing the "Kurdistan project in a challenging war" environment (AR FY15, p.24) [M]; the FY16 AR lists "Decrease in oil prices" among threats (AR FY16, p.19) [M]; by FY17 "the fixed assets were in Iraq where the subsidiary Company no longer has any presence and considering the high transportation [cost]…" (AR FY17, p.137) [A]. Equipment was stranded at "Gas Qalafa Factory Street, Old Kalar, Sulaymaniyah" (AR FY16, p.109) [A]. Samara sold 56.32% to Oilmax Energy under an SPA; Oilmax became promoter on 5-Aug-2016 (AR FY17, p.33) [A].
- Lesson: an overseas single-customer spread with debt-funded equipment produced two years of revenue and then a write-down and a change of control. The present "Kuiper + international O&M" plan should be read against this.

### Cycle 3 – FY17–FY20: Oilmax rebuild — in reality an African EPC/O&M cycle, not a seismic cycle

- Revenue: FY17 124, FY18 222, FY19 194, FY20 273 (model) [A]. EBITDA margin FY18 18%, FY19 17%, FY20 24% (model) [A].
- What the market was told the driver was: domestic seismic for ONGC/OIL/Vedanta under NSP/OALP. Jun-2017 deck: seismic order book ~Rs.503cr (Myanmar 48, Mizoram 79, Arunachal 61, NC Hills 64, Manipur 143) and "10 crews which are currently operational" (PPT Jun2017, p.403, p.231) [M].
- What the audited major-customer note shows the driver actually was: one overseas customer each year.
  - FY17: top customer Rs.63.5cr = 51% of revenue; top three 93% (AR FY18, p.143) [A].
  - FY18: top customer Rs.126.6cr = 57%; top three 89%; four customers >10% (AR FY18, p.143) [A]. The Jun-2017 deck shows the only large O&M contract was Koral Energy's FPU EBOK, Nigeria, "Duration 5 years (3+1+1), Contract value approx USD 95 [million]" (PPT Jun2017) [M]. Koral terminated the contract in May 2018 (Press Release 4QFY18) [M].
  - FY19: top customer Rs.99.5cr = 51%; three >10% (AR FY19, p.153) [A]. New award: Amni International, Nigeria, USD 52m MOPU upgrade ("Langley") (Press Release 4QFY19) [M].
  - FY20: top customer Rs.238.6cr = 87% of Rs.273cr; top three 99%; only one customer >10% (AR FY20, p.173; AR FY21, p.157) [A]. The 3QFY20 call confirms which: "Ongoing execution of Langley Turnaround Maintenance project in Nigeria… has primarily contributed to the robust performance" (Q3FY20 call) [M]; Q2FY20 revenue was "Almost 90% from EPC and production facility business. And very minimal Rs.2 Crore, from seismic" (Q2FY20 call) [M].
- So the FY20 record year (24% EBITDA, Rs.29cr PAT, debt-free) was one Nigerian turnaround job. Domestic seismic in those years was repeatedly interrupted: "loss of 45 operational days" to rain, Manipur "work progress… negligible" (Press Release 4QFY18) [M]; NC Hills and Manipur "under force majeure" (Q2FY20 call) [M]; Oil India encashed a Rs.12.5cr performance guarantee on Manipur (Q3FY20 call) [M]; the Bashneft Iraq order (USD 37m, ~Rs.260cr) received Q1FY20 was excluded from the order book and never executed (Q3FY20 call; PPT Dec2019, p.143) [M].
- The seismic promise of the cycle: a Rs.570cr (ex-GST), six-block, two-year 2D/3D callout contract in Rajasthan and Gujarat received Q3FY20 from "a reputed Indian Oil and Gas Company" (Q3FY20 call) [M]; later identified as Vedanta — "AESL has successfully completed current phase of Rajasthan and Cambay Seismic project for Vedanta Ltd" (Press Release 4QFY22) [M]. Capex of Rs.70–75cr was announced for it (Q3FY20 call) [M].
- Lesson: the rebuild narrative was "most economical solution provider… entire value chain"; the P&L was a single African client whose receivable then went bad (Cycle 4). Management's own target — "by FY23, we do not have a single business or client which is contributing more than 30 to 40% of our total revenue" (Q2FY20 call) [M] — was set precisely because of this concentration.

### Cycle 4 – FY21–FY23: Nigeria suspension, seismic stall, FY23 collapse

- Numbers: FY21 229 (O&G 222 / Mineral 7), FY22 260 (189 / 72), FY23 110 (63 / 47); segment results FY21 78 / –4, FY22 55 / 33, FY23 –0.3 / 6.7 (model; AR FY22, p.172; AR FY23, p.88) [A]. FY23 loss Rs.44.4cr (AR FY23, p.10) [A].
- FY21: Amni "issued notice of suspension of work effective November 16, 2020, on account of certain technical challenges faced by AMNI… Trade receivables includes Rs.6,247.35 Lacs (USD 8,499,254) receivable from AMNI" (AR FY21, p.156, Note 41) [A]. Auditor qualified the consolidated accounts for USD 2.11m unconfirmed (AGM Sep-2021 transcript) [M]. ADMCC "issued notice of termination… dated August 03, 2021" (AR FY22, p.176) [A]. Revenue booked on Langley was "approx. 75%" of a contract that had grown to "approximately USD 65 million" (Press Release 4QFY21) [M]. Top customer FY21 Rs.105.8cr = 46%; three customers >10% (AR FY21, p.157) [A].
- FY22: the year the two new legs were signed — Vedanta Suvali integrated O&M, Rs.248cr incl. GST, four years + one, from Apr-2022 (Order 24-Jan-2022) [M]; Hura CHP, Rs.128cr incl. GST, with JV partner, 3 Mtpa, EPC + 5-year O&M (Order 14-Feb-2022) [M]; Gevra CHP expansion for HEC (sub-contract), Rs.236cr (PPT 4QFY24, p.901) [M]. Four customers >10% (AR FY22, p.173) [A].
- FY23 collapse — what the filings say, not what the market assumed:
  1. Seismic dried up. "Presently, for seismic, we have around Rs.60 crores of contract on hand… there has been no activity for the last 5-6 months in terms of the tendering" (Q1FY23 call, CEO and CFO) [M]. "we were expecting a certain contract award by the Government of India of NSP-2 which was expected to kick-start working in May and June month, but those contracts have been delayed by the government" (Q1FY23 call) [M]. Seismic is "driven by government policies… not actually directly dependent on crude oil" (Q1FY23 call) [M].
  2. The seismic that existed was weather- and site-hit: "interruption in ongoing seismic projects in Assam due to early arrival of monsoon… We effectively lost a month" (Q1FY23 call) [M]; "disruption of seismic projects in Tripura due to local issues… have short closed Oil India Limited Tripura project in consultation with the client" (AR FY23, p.6, Chairman's letter) [M]. Campaigns completed that year were small: "seismic campaigns in Chhattisgarh's Rajadai… contracted by Vedanta in Assam's Jagun" (AR FY23, p.8) [M].
  3. CHP phasing. "slowdown of Gevra project in Chhattisgarh"; Hura "has finally kicked up this quarter… after getting the necessary clearance for the land acquisition" (Q1FY23 call) [M]; "we encountered delays in the progress of the Gevra project due to external factors" (AR FY23, p.6) [M].
  4. Costs were kept: "we have hired a lot of people for doing that O&M contract… still we continue to employ few people in anticipation of making good recovery" (Q1FY23 call) [M]. Oil & gas segment result went to –Rs.0.3cr on Rs.63cr.
  5. Was there a dispute? Not with Vedanta or ONGC in FY23: Suvali O&M ran from 1-Apr-2022 and was later extended to Mar-2027 "on the same rates, terms and conditions" (Order 16-Mar-2026) [M]. The live disputes were Amni (termination Aug-2021, receivable) and, later, ONGC's Sep-2024 "holiday" order over the 2017 and 2018 seismic contracts (Order 20-Sep-2024) [M]. The Vedanta Rs.570cr six-block contract was a callout structure; the AR records completion of the "current phase" (Press Release 4QFY22) and there is no disclosure of the remaining callouts being issued — the order simply faded from the order book (Rs.60cr seismic by Aug-2022). Not disclosed: how much of the Rs.570cr was ultimately called out.
  6. Rating: Ind-Ra cut to IND BBB- (Feb-2023) and moved the outlook to Negative (Aug-2023) (Credit Rating 10-Feb-2023; 15-Aug-2023) [S]. Equity: Rs.85cr warrants (Jun-2023) (Press Release 30-Jun-2023) [M].
- What the market believed: investors on the Q1FY23 call were working to "Rs.400-500 crores expectation of top line for the year" and a Rs.100cr O&M run-rate (Q1FY23 call, P. Diwan) [M]; management replied the value erosion "is temporary" and "Q3 and Q4 will be significantly better" (Q1FY23 call) [M]. FY23 revenue was Rs.110cr; EBITDA turned positive only in Q1FY24 "after 4 quarters" (Press Release 1QFY24) [M].
- Lesson: when seismic tenders stop, the fixed cost of a standing seismic organisation plus the cost of staffing new verticals ahead of revenue produces a loss within two quarters. FY23 is the stress test for the current cost base.

## R6. Value chain and business model

### 6.1 Chains, line by line (text diagrams)

Seismic data acquisition (India):
Inputs [survey permits, land access and crop compensation → geophysicists/observers (own) + ~100s of local labour per crew (hired) → recording channels, geophones, vibroseis or shot-hole drilling rigs (own, 74% depreciated) + hired vehicles/machinery → fuel, camps, explosives] → Process [line clearance, drilling/vibrating, recording, field QC; monsoon shut-down 3–4 months in NE/Gujarat] → Product [2D line-km / 3D sq-km of seismic data, with processing bought in or sub-contracted] → Customer [ONGC, Oil India, Vedanta, Sun Petrochemicals, GAIL, CMPDIL; tender/L1] → End user [the operator's exploration programme; the Government via NSP/OALP/Mission Anveshan].

Oilfield O&M / integrated field development:
Inputs [operations engineers, technicians, HSE staff (own, posted at site) → spares and consumables (reimbursable or priced in) → sub-contracted drilling rigs, workover, logistics, process-plant fabrication] → Process [take over a facility/field; run it to uptime and production KPIs; for Vedanta, drill wells and build facilities under an approved FDP] → Product [facility availability; incremental barrels] → Customer [Vedanta (Suvali; Rajasthan satellite fields), Oilmax (Amguri), Hardy/Svetah (PY-3), AGCL (compressor BOOT)] → End user [the operator's production and the state's royalty].

Coal handling plant EPC (and O&M):
Inputs [design engineering (own) → structural steel, conveyors, crushers, silos, electricals (bought; "almost all the material is domestic supplied", Q4FY26 call) → civil works and erection (sub-contracted) → project managers (own)] → Process [18–24 month build on an operating mine, weather-dependent civil] → Product [commissioned CHP/rapid loading system; then 5-year O&M or DLP O&M] → Customer [ECL/CCL/MCL/SCCL/HEC/GSECL via the JV/AOP] → End user [Coal India's first-mile connectivity programme; railways/power plants].

Kuiper manpower:
Inputs [database of offshore drilling, marine, EPCI personnel; visas, mobilisation, insurance; local sponsor entities (Qatar 49%, Indonesia 49%)] → Process [recruit, mobilise, payroll under multi-year master service agreements] → Product [day-rate crews] → Customer ["top-tier EPCI, drilling and other energy service companies" in KSA, Qatar, UAE, SE Asia, Africa] → End user [NOCs and IOCs' offshore programmes].

Indrora/Mevad (PI owner): inputs [wells (CWIP), Oilmax as operator, sharing of producing expense Rs.0.7cr FY26] → crude sold to IOC on a dated-Brent formula (Q4FY26 call) [M] → Government revenue share Rs.0.48cr (Note 51a) [A].

Actual FY26 debtor days 132, 209 including unbilled (model) [A].

### 6.3 Where value and IP sit; bought-in vs in-house
- In-house: seismic crew management and QC (the historical differentiator — "first Indian company to adopt wireless technology", IAGC/ISO certification), facility operations teams, CHP design and project management, bid preparation, Oilmax's subsurface/drilling know-how post-merger.
- Bought in: seismic processing (partly), all CHP equipment and civil works, drilling rigs and workover units, vessels, most hired machinery; Kuiper's product is itself bought-in labour.
- Certifications/approvals: "ISO Certified Company and IAGC Certified Contractor"; "Pre-Qualified by Majors and Independents such as Petronas, Total, Bashneft, ONGC, OIL, Jubilant, Reliance, Essar and others" (PPT Jun2017) [M]; Oil India three-year empanelment for onshore 2D/3D (Jun-2026) — "Contract awards will be subject to separate tendering processes" (Order 5-Jun-2026) [M]; ONGC tender ban Sep-2024 to Mar-2025 (Orders 20-Sep-2024, 10-Feb-2025) [M]; for MDO or offshore work AESL needs partners — "we will definitely try to tie up with some technical partner" (Q2FY25 call), "discussing collaboration agreements with companies who will give us qualification to participate in seismic businesses… offshore" (AGM Sep-2021) [M].
- Position in chain and who captures margin: AESL is a tier-1 contractor to NOCs/large privates in seismic and O&M, but in CHP it is frequently a JV lead billing an AOP, and in Gevra it was HEC's sub-contractor. The operator captures the resource rent; AESL earns a 15–25% operating margin on India services and ~7% on Kuiper. There is no proprietary technology; the wireless-seismic edge of 2013–2020 has not been renewed (equipment additions Rs.0.9cr in FY26).

## R7. Moat, in layers

Scepticism up front: this is a contracting business in which every large contract to date has come from an open tender or a negotiated single award, several have ended in dispute (Koral, Amni, ONGC holiday, Oil India PBG), and the CRISIL rationale cites "exposure to intense competition, government regulations and policies, and the working capital-intensive nature of operations" (Credit Rating 10-Sep-2025) [S]. ### R10.5 Cyclicality and lumpiness (quantified)

- AESL revenue (consolidated, Rs cr): FY21 229; FY22 260; FY23 110; FY24 305; FY25 465; FY26 791 [A, Screener series of audited accounts]. FY23 fell 58% when ONGC Tripura work was force-majeured and "seismic order flow ... was way below (almost negligible)" (AR FY2022-23 p.6, Chairman's letter [M]); seismic order book was Rs 60 cr in Jul-2022 (Transcript 1QFY23 [M]) vs Rs 1,343 cr total order book with 63% seismic in Dec-2019 [M].
- AESL seismic revenue: ~Rs 120 cr FY24 -> Rs 72 cr FY25 (-40%) [M]; seismic was 8% of order book (Rs 74 cr) at Mar-2025 and 4.4% at Sep-2025 [M, PPT 4QFY25; Transcript 2QFY26].
- Alphageo revenue (Rs cr): FY17 306; FY18 429; FY19 405; FY20 268; FY21 143; FY22 155; FY23 66; FY24 99; FY25 126; FY26 112 — an 85% peak-to-trough fall over FY18-FY23 as NSP ended [S, Screener].
- ONGC 3D: 8,588 SKM (FY24) -> 8,840 (FY25) -> 4,631 (FY26); 2D: 972 -> 604 -> 957 LKM [S].
- Management's own characterisation: "seismic orders are driven by government policies and government decisions ... crude oil price increase probably will not affect us" (Transcript 1QFY23, CEO [M]); seismic has monsoon seasonality (no operations in Q2) [M, PPT Dec2019].
- CHP tendering also pauses: "in last 6 months there was definitely a slowdown into the tendering activities" (May-2025) then "picked up" (Nov-2025) [M, Transcripts 4QFY25, 2QFY26].
Where we are: domestic onshore seismic is at a cyclical low in awards (ONGC 3D halved in FY26; both listed contractors loss-making or shrinking in seismic), with a policy-driven upturn promised but mostly offshore. CHP is mid-cycle: ~52% of FMC projects commissioned, award cadence lumpy but the project count keeps being extended. Gulf crewing is recovering from the 2024 Aramco rig suspensions but exposed to the 2026 West Asia conflict.

### R10.6 Long-dated structural threats (horizon)

- Exploration budgets: IEA WEI 2026 shows global upstream oil investment falling for a third year and Middle East O&G investment -1% in 2026; gas investment at a 10-year high [S]. India is counter-cyclical (ONGC +20% exploration capex FY27; Samudra Manthan) but the incremental rupee is offshore. Horizon: 2026-2031 for the Samudra Manthan window; onshore contractor demand depends on Mission Anveshan successors that do not yet exist.
- Coal: IEA Coal 2025 projects India coal demand +17% by 2030 (~3% p.a.; non-power use 356 -> 470 Mt) while global demand peaks in 2025 and falls ~3% by 2030 [S, IEA via sxcoal/petromindo Dec-2025]; India targets 1.31 bt output in FY27 and CIL 1 bt by FY30 [S/M]. CHP demand therefore persists to ~2030-32 (80 GW new coal capacity to 300 GW by FY32 [M, Transcript 4QFY25 p.2]), after which FMC becomes a replacement/O&M market. A coal-demand peak in India is not in any official forecast before 2035.
- Substitution in seismic: ONGC in-house capacity (24,000 new channels, 2026 season); nodal/wireless systems lowering crew intensity; airborne geophysics for inaccessible NSP areas (via OIL) [S]. Permanent reservoir monitoring and drone surveys: no Indian onshore deployment found.
- Energy-transition tail risk for Kuiper: Gulf NOCs' capex is being redirected to gas/LNG and (ADNOC) low-carbon; crewing demand follows rig counts, which are set by OPEC+ policy rather than by energy transition over a 5-year horizon [S].

### R11.2 Basis of competition, where AESL wins/loses, substitution and bargaining power

- Tender mechanics: PSU (ONGC/OIL/CIL/SCCL) contracts are two-stage technical pre-qualification (crew/equipment experience, HSE, past similar work) followed by L1 price; AESL "declared L1" language is used for every pending award [M, Transcripts 2QFY25, 4QFY26]. OIL's 3-year empanelment (Jun-2026) is pre-qualification only [A]. Private operators (Vedanta, Sun Petro, Hardy/HOEC) negotiate and repeat-award [M].
- Where AESL wins: CHP (>50% hit rate, 7-year contracts with O&M tail, few credible bidders after Tecpro/McNally exits); Vedanta O&M (incumbent since 2022, extended 2026); Mission Anveshan 2D in Rajasthan (desert terrain, vibroseis owned since 2020 [M, Press Release 13-Jul-2020]). Where it loses or is absent: ONGC/OIL seismic where it wins ~1 in 3; offshore seismic (no vessel); Chinese-contractor-priced export markets (Iraq Bashneft project stalled 2019-20 [M, PPT Dec2019]).
- Substitution risk: ONGC in-house parties (new Sercel channels) are the main substitute for onshore 3D; nodal/wireless technology lowers barriers for new entrants (AESL was first Indian user of wireless, ~16,000 channels in 2019 [M]); for CHP, CIL could re-let as MDO-bundled scope (Power Mech, Sical, Caliber Mining win mining-cum-logistics packages [S]).
- Supplier power: recording systems are a Sercel (Viridien)/INOVA/Wireless Seismic oligopoly; AESL lists them as "affiliations" [M, PPT 4QFY24 slide 20]; ONGC buying 24,000 channels directly from Sercel shows the supplier will sell around the contractor. CHP EPC inputs (structural steel, conveyors, Elecon/TRF/Macawber equipment) are competitive; JV partners (Indwell, Zuberi) share margin.
- Customer power and terms: CIL subsidiaries hold retention money released on mechanical completion — retention rose from Rs 23-24 cr (FY24) to Rs 55 cr of Rs 224 cr receivables (FY25), with "only Rs 14 cr" >90 days excluding retention; ~Rs 30 cr retention release expected FY26 [M, Transcript 4QFY25 p.11]. LD clauses and advance/BG terms are not disclosed in filings (AR FY26 contains no retention/LD disclosure beyond standard revenue-recognition notes [A]); CRISIL's 2025 rationale lists "intense competition, government regulations-policy and working capital intensive nature of operations" as offsets [S, CRISIL 06-Aug-2025 and 10-Sep-2025 filing]. Payment delays by CIL subsidiaries are evidenced by the Rs 60 cr jump in CHP receivables in FY25 [M].
- Customer concentration: CIL group >20% of revenue if consolidated; no single contract >20% of order book [M, Transcript 4QFY25 p.14].

Measured from the FY23 trough the return looks good, but that is recovery of EBIT that existed in FY20-FY22, not new return on new capital. Within the Rs.406cr: receivables and unbilled +Rs.356cr, Kuiper Rs.123cr (returning perhaps Rs.21cr of EBITDA), Indrora Rs.82cr (returning Rs.0.5cr of crude sales net), offset by payables +Rs.166cr.

### R13.3 Verdict

Capital has not been well allocated, judged by what owners have received for what they put in. Over FY17-FY26 shareholders supplied Rs.270cr (plus Rs.92cr in May-2026) and were paid Rs.4.4cr; the company's cumulative free cash flow to the firm is -Rs.110cr; the share count is up 2.2x (and will roughly double again under the merger); ROE has not exceeded 12.5% in the growth years. The specific decisions that look poor on the record: (1) equity issued to the promoter and others at Rs.127.50 in 2023 when the business already had Rs.40cr of cash, converted at a 60-70% discount to market; (2) Rs.82cr diverted into a 27-bopd oil interest bought from the promoter; (3) Rs.6.3cr into an EV start-up, written off; (4) Rs.36cr of cumulative losses in two offshore subsidiaries that produced the Nigeria write-off and three qualified audits; (5) a dividend started while raising equity. The decisions that may yet prove right: Kuiper (bought at 0.75x fair-value book with Rs.72cr of cash inside, if its 7% margin and Rs.300cr of annual revenue are durable and the Middle East holds) and the CHP/EPC order book (Rs.1,754cr) if its receivables and retentions are collected. The test for the next two years is simple: CFO must exceed PAT without fixed-deposit movements, and the new Rs.158cr of debt must be repaid from operations rather than refinanced with equity.

### Real red flags versus structural quirks

**Red flags (things that should change a valuation or require explanation):**
1. **Promoter-as-counterparty on both sides of the ledger.** Oilmax is the biggest single related customer (Rs.27.8 crore), operator of AESL's only oil asset, guarantor of its bank lines, prospective Rs.1,000 crore mine-development client, and the company whose Rs.4,349/share DCF value (no market check possible) sets the merger ratio that dilutes minorities from 39% to 48% of a company with no institutional owner. Approvals were obtained (independent committee, fairness opinion, majority-of-minority vote), but the economics rest on a promoter-supplied forecast of fields that are mostly not yet producing.
2. **Executive churn at the operating core before the merger**: COO (9 months), VP Infra (Jul-2025), and the heads of Seismic, O&M and Procurement (Aug/Sep-2026), with no disclosed successors.
3. **Finance and reporting footprint vs. auditor capacity**: a small Mumbai audit firm now signs a group with ~25 foreign entities, 15 of them unaudited, several held through nominee/POA arrangements in Qatar, Malaysia and Indonesia, and a database-level audit trail that was not enabled.
4. **Compensation design**: MD pay +76% to 51x median with shareholder approval to exceed SEBI limits; Rs.10-strike options; options to non-executive directors and to holding-company staff.
5. **Disclosure cadence**: half-yearly calls, three-year gaps in the past, and a 2-year PSU tender ban that was disclosed but never explained beyond "technical grounds".

**Structural quirks (uncomfortable but explainable, and priced):**
- Large JV receivables and JV-routed revenue are a consequence of Coal India pre-qualification rules, not evidence of leakage; the JV profits are audited and small.
- Dormant offshore shells with negative net worth (DMCC, AOSL Petroleum) are Samara-era residue; the cash is long gone and the exposure is capped.
- Personal and corporate guarantees by the promoter for bank lines are normal for an Indian mid-cap and, if anything, show skin in the game.
- Zero contingent liabilities and a clean CARO are genuinely good.
- The two-CFO structure, family directors and Oilmax-named JVs are features of a founder group that is in the middle of collapsing a holding structure into one listed company; the merger removes the holding-company discount and the inter-company billing with Oilmax, which is the right direction even if the price of admission is dilution.

## R18. Growth drivers and strategy

### Management's stated strategy

Three statements define it. First, the board's 9-Mar-2024 press release: move "from cyclical business which also frequently involves force majeure situations, to stable business with long term contracts" [M]. ## R19. Risks and triggers

### Risks, most damaging first

1. **The merger imports a promoter company whose earnings have already fallen.** Oilmax's standalone PAT was Rs.48.6cr in FY25 on operating revenue of Rs.127.7cr; for the nine months to Dec-2025 it reported a loss of Rs.12.2cr on revenue of Rs.71.7cr, after a Rs.33.8cr ESOP charge and with pre-ESOP profit before tax of about Rs.21.6cr (OEPL 3-year financials; OEPL unaudited Dec-2025) [A]. The swap of 117 AESL shares per 10 Oilmax shares was set in Sep-2025 on a DCF value of Rs.4,349 per Oilmax share, about Rs.2,480cr for the company, of which about Rs.1,015cr is Oilmax's own stake in AESL at the Rs.372 reference price; the implied value of the E&P business was therefore about Rs.1,460cr, roughly 30 times its FY25 profit (Merger Valuation Report, p.10-11) [S]. At Rs.455 the market pays about Rs.3,030cr for the 6.67cr new shares, of which about Rs.1,240cr is the cancelled AESL stake and about Rs.1,790cr is the E&P business. Public holders' claim on AESL's own earnings falls from about 44% to about 24% of the enlarged 8.80cr shares; for the merger to be neutral to them on FY26 numbers, Oilmax must earn at least about Rs.42cr of PAT a year [D]. How we would lose: Amguri, Tiphuk and Duarmara production disappoints (both new fields have already slipped from "FY26" to "FY27"), Oilmax earns less than Rs.42cr, and the enlarged share count de-rates. What would tell us: Oilmax's audited FY26 accounts, the first post-merger E&P segment disclosure, and monthly production numbers in the decks.

2. **Cash conversion and the next equity raise.** Receivables plus unbilled were Rs.512cr at FY26, 209 days of sales, including Rs.95cr of retention, Rs.164cr of unbilled contract assets and Rs.91cr owed by AESL's own CHP joint ventures (AR FY26, Notes 14, 17, 46C) [A]. Three-year operating cash flow is negative Rs.24cr against Rs.119cr of PAT; FY26's Rs.53cr includes Rs.20cr of fixed-deposit redemptions and Kuiper's opening working capital (R12.3). Gross debt went from Rs.24cr to Rs.158cr. The company has raised equity in every growth phase (Rs.270cr since FY17 plus Rs.92cr in May-2026) and paid Rs.4.4cr of dividends. How we would lose: CHP collections and retention release slip, bank lines fill, and a further preferential or warrant issue dilutes at a price below today's. What would tell us: operating cash flow before fixed-deposit movements in H1FY27 (Nov-2026); receivable plus unbilled days; any board notice for a fundraise.

3. **Kuiper's margin and the Gulf rig cycle.** Kuiper is a crew-supply business with crew cost at 86% of revenue and a 7% EBITDA margin [A/M]; its revenue fell from USD 82m to USD 68m between 2022 and 2024 when Aramco suspended jackups, and Qatar operations were disrupted in Mar-2026 (R10, R12). It is now 38% of consolidated revenue and the reason consolidated margin fell to 12.1% in FY26 and 8.1% in 1QFY27. How we would lose: a second rig-suspension round or a wider West Asia conflict cuts Kuiper revenue 15-20% while AESL carries the USD 6.7m acquisition loan. What would tell us: Kuiper quarterly revenue (consolidated less standalone) and the consolidated margin against the 12-13% guide; Aramco and ADNOC rig counts.

4. **Concentration in two contracts and one private customer.** The Vedanta integrated contract is about 40% of the order book and MCL Lakhanpur about 26% [D]; the top three customers were 33% of FY26 revenue and three customers each exceeded 10% [A]. Vedanta's own production fell 16% in FY26 and its capex is being re-prioritised (R10). Every prior cycle ended when one or two awards ended (R4). How we would lose: a slower Vedanta well programme or a dispute on the integrated contract removes the single largest growth driver; the FY23 template shows the cost base does not flex. What would tell us: wells drilled and facilities milestones in the quarterly decks; Vedanta's capex disclosures; any Reg 30 filing on contract variation.

5. **Execution and guidance credibility.** Twelve of 27 scored promises were missed, including the FY26 standalone guide by 24-30% on revenue (R16). No CHP commissioning has been announced for Hura (LoA Feb-2022), Singareni or Gevra despite three sets of dates. The heads of Seismic, O&M and Procurement resigned in Aug-Sep 2026 with no disclosed successors, following the COO's nine-month tenure and the VP Infra's exit in Jul-2025 (R14). How we would lose: the FY27 standalone guide of +30-40% is missed again and the stock de-rates from 40x trailing. What would tell us: 2QFY27 and 3QFY27 standalone revenue against the guide; commissioning filings; replacement appointments.

6. **Related-party and governance structure.** The promoter is customer (Rs.27.8cr of FY26 sales), operator of AESL's only oil asset, guarantor of its bank lines, landlord, and prospective Rs.1,000cr mine-development counterparty under an approved RPT ceiling (AR FY26, AOC-2; Note 46) [A]. Post merger the promoter group holds 49.45% on the post-conversion share count (Merger Notice, post-scheme pattern) and the family's second generation sits on the committee that sets options. The group's auditor changed to a smaller firm while 15 Kuiper subsidiaries went unaudited (R15). How we would lose: value migrates to the promoter through pricing of intra-group contracts or further option grants, and minority investors cannot see it until the annual report. What would tell us: the RPT schedule in each annual report; audit committee disclosures; ESOP grants; any emphasis of matter.

7. **Structural decline in the original business.** Outsourced onshore seismic in India has run at about Rs.250-400cr a year of awards, both listed contractors are shrinking or loss-making in it, ONGC bought 24,000 in-house channels for the 2026 season, and AESL added Rs.0.9cr of seismic equipment in FY26 to a fleet that is 74% depreciated (R10, R11, R8). How we would lose: not directly, since seismic is 5% of the book, but the equipment has no replacement value and any revival in demand would need capex the company is not making. What would tell us: seismic order intake; ONGC tender outcomes.

8. **Analytical risks (what cannot be verified from public documents).** Kuiper's standalone profit and loss and balance sheet (Ind AS 103 disclosure absent); Oilmax's reserves (the "about 70 million barrels" is a merger-deck figure with no certified report in the filing set) and its FY26 audited accounts; which contracts ended in FY23; whether Hura, Singareni and Gevra are commissioned; where the AGCL BOOT capex sits; the loan covenants; the outcome of the PY-3 escalation claim; the counterparty of the Rs.2.7cr FY26 write-off; and the reasons for the Aug-Sep 2026 resignations. Each is a question the model cannot answer and a place where the thesis can be wrong without warning.

9. **Long-dated structural risks.** Coal India's first-mile-connectivity pipeline tails off after FY29-30 unless the project count keeps rising, after which CHP becomes an O&M and replacement market; ONGC's incremental exploration rupee is offshore where AESL cannot bid; Gulf crewing demand follows OPEC+ rig policy; India's coal demand peak is not in any official forecast before 2035 (R10.6). Horizon: 2029 onward.

## R20. Preliminary view

**What the market seems to believe.** At Rs.455 (16-Oct-2026) the stock trades at about 40x FY26 EPS and 21x FY26 EV/EBITDA, and about Rs.2,200cr of market value on the post-conversion share count (R17). That price treats AESL as a diversified, integrated energy-services compounder: a Rs.1,754cr order book growing 30-40% a year at improving margins, a cheaply bought international platform in Kuiper, and free optionality on a producing E&P portfolio arriving through the Oilmax merger. The 2024-26 re-rating from Rs.110 to Rs.455 was driven by order announcements, the Kuiper deal, the merger and the 1QFY27 revenue print.

**What the evidence suggests.** The business is a contractor with a 12% EBITDA margin, an 11.5% ROE, negative three-year operating cash flow and a share count that has doubled in ten years and is about to double again. Every cycle in its history has rested on one or two contracts and ended when they did; the current cycle rests on Vedanta and MCL in the same way. The original seismic business is in structural decline and is not being reinvested in. Kuiper is a 7%-margin payroll pass-through bought well but exposed to Gulf rig counts. Guidance has been missed in most years it was given. The merger, approved on FY25 numbers, brings in a company that reported a nine-month loss before the shareholder vote, and it is the largest single determinant of where the stock goes from here. The incremental return on the Rs.406cr of capital added since FY20 is 8.6% pre-tax.

**Variant perception.** The market is paying a growth multiple for a business whose growth is order-book-driven, working-capital-hungry and funded by equity, and it is paying it before the merger's earnings dilution is visible in a reported quarter. The variant view is that FY27 reported EPS on 8.80cr shares will be lower than the market's implied Rs.18-23 unless Oilmax earns Rs.40cr or more, and that the first two post-merger results will reset expectations. The offsetting truths: the CHP franchise has few credible competitors after Tecpro and McNally left, the Vedanta relationship is real and expanding, Kuiper came with Rs.72cr of cash for a Rs.123cr outlay, and Indian onshore services demand is policy-supported for the next three to four years. There is a decent business inside a poor capital-allocation record and an expensive stock.

**Falsification test.** We would abandon the cautious view if, by the H1FY27 results in Nov-2026, standalone revenue is growing at 30% or more with operating cash flow before fixed-deposit movements positive and receivable plus unbilled days below about 170; and if Oilmax's FY26 audited PAT before ESOP cost is Rs.40cr or more with Tiphuk or Duarmara on production by Mar-2027. We would abandon any constructive view if a further equity raise is announced within twelve months of the May-2026 conversion, or if Oilmax reports a second consecutive loss.

**Questions the model must answer (Stage 3).**
1. What standalone revenue and EBITDA do the committed contracts support in FY27-FY29, contract by contract (Vedanta Rs.772cr over 57 months, MCL, GSECL, AGCL, Suvali to Mar-2027, seismic in hand), and what has to come from unsigned work to reach the guide?
2. At 120-130 net working capital days, how much cash does 30% standalone growth absorb each year, and does gross debt rise or fall from Rs.158cr without new equity?
3. What is Kuiper worth as a USD 55-65m, 7-9% margin crew-supply business with SOFR-linked debt, and what happens to consolidated EPS if its revenue falls 20%?
4. What production, realisation and cost does Oilmax need to earn Rs.42cr of PAT, and what are the five blocks worth on a DCF or NAV basis with reserves that are not independently certified in our document set?
5. On 8.80cr shares, what pro forma FY27 and FY28 EPS does the base case produce, and what multiple is justified for a 12-14% margin contractor with 12% ROE against peers that will be supplied with Bloomberg data?
