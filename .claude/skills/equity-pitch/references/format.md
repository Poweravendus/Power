# Research note format - section-by-section specification

The format below is derived from the analyst's Arrow Greentech note. Section order,
tables, and voice are fixed; the content of each section adapts to the company.
Worked examples are quoted from that note to calibrate tone - do not reuse their
facts.

## 1. Title block

Line 1 - company name + short thematic tagline
(e.g. "Arrow Greentech Limited - The Invisible Thread").

Line 2 - one line, pipe-separated:
`NSE: TICKER | CMP: Rs.X | Market Cap: Rs.Xcr | Rating: BUY/HOLD/SELL | Target Price: Rs.X | Upside: X%`

Line 3 - month + year.

Rating and TP come from the Valuation section's base case - write this block last.

## 2. Company Overview

3-4 bullets:
- What the company makes/does, for whom, where it manufactures, flagship products,
  and key subsidiaries.
- Origin story: founder/promoter background, patents/IP, listing status.
- Latest-year performance snapshot with the one-line explanation of any sharp move
  ("FY26 revenue fell 18%... driven simultaneously by RBI's indent declining 24%
  and disruption in export order flows").
- Where the stock trades now on the chosen forward multiple.

## 3. Revenue & EBITDA Margin - Business Cycles

The signature section. Segment the company's **full listed history** into named
cycles (Cycle 1, Cycle 2, ...), each with:
- Year range and a one-line label ("Cycle 3 - FY13-17: The royalty boom - the most
  misunderstood cycle")
- The revenue driver, cost structure, and margin profile of that era
- What actually happened, sourced from the ARs of those years (quote the AR where
  a single sentence proves the point)
- What the cycle teaches about the current thesis

End with the current/next projected cycle and its assumptions. Open the section by
stating why the cycle lens matters for the thesis (e.g. "the FY26 decline is Cycle
5's trough, not the beginning of a new collapse").

## 4. Business Segments

Open with how the company reports segments (Ind AS 108) and what physically
distinguishes them.

**Segment revenue table** - Rs. crore; columns = last 4 actual years + 4 estimate
years; rows = revenue and YoY per segment. Footnote the margin basis.

Then one sub-section per segment (numbered, with % of latest-year revenue in the
heading): what it sells, to whom, market size and CAGR with source, margin
behaviour under stress, capacity position, and the constraint on growth
(realisation-led vs volume-led, what unlocks volume).

## 5. Business Model & Value Chain

- The value chain in plain language: raw material (and its supply/currency risk),
  process, where the IP physically resides, end markets, certifications.
- The moat in layers: qualification barriers (time and audits required), patent
  portfolio (jurisdictions, recency), accumulated know-how / switching costs.
- Capital intensity: gross block vs revenue (asset turns), maintenance capex,
  and what that implies for self-funding of growth.

## 6. Investment Thesis

3-4 numbered pillars. Each: a bold one-line claim, then one support paragraph of
verifiable numbers. Prefer checkable claims over narrative optimism - the register
of the Arrow pillars is the target:
- "FY26 was a verifiable cyclical trough - priced as permanent"
- "Margin recovery is mechanical, not assumed"
- "Treasury of Rs.103cr earns 7% - not in the price"

## 7. Quarterly Trend

Table, Rs. crore: last 8 quarters as columns; rows = Revenue, YoY, EBITDA, EBITDA
margin, PAT. One short paragraph on what the quarterly cadence shows (seasonality,
run-rate vs annual estimate, inflection evidence). If the company reports only
half-yearly (SME listings), present the last 4 halves and say so.

## 8. Financial Performance

Table, Rs. crore: columns = last 5 actual years + 4 estimate years; rows =
Revenue, YoY growth, EBITDA, EBITDA Margin, PAT, PAT Margin, EPS (Rs./share),
DPS (Rs./share). Footnote: "Source: Audited consolidated financials FYxx-FYxxA.
FYxx-FYxxE: analyst estimates." (or "per user's model" as applicable).

## 9. Treasury & Balance Sheet Summary

Net-cash company: table of Current Investments, Cash & Bank, Total Treasury,
Treasury per share, Shareholders' Funds across last 5 actuals + 2 estimate years.
Leveraged company: replace with the debt profile - gross/net debt, cost, maturity,
covenants, and the deleveraging path. Either way, one paragraph on what the balance
sheet enables or constrains.

## 10. Stock Price History - Price and Business Story

Narrate the price chart in named phases, each phase linking price to what the
business was actually doing per the ARs of those years ("The wilderness (2018-21,
Rs.80-250): three years of losses, zero analyst coverage... This is when the
security thread plant was being built - real value being created with no price
discovery"). Close with the current disconnect or confirmation: what today's
price implies vs the business reality. Note the price data source and that prices
are approximate monthly closes.

## 11. Valuation

- Choose the multiple (default EV/EBITDA; justify if different) and name listed
  comparables - global and Indian - with their current multiples and why each is
  or isn't a fair anchor.
- Scenario table: rows = consolidated EBITDA, net debt (negative = net cash),
  implied multiple at CMP, then target price at three multiple levels (state which
  is base); columns = latest actual + estimate years.
- Close with one line: "Base TP Rs.X (Yx FYxxE) = Z% upside. Bull case Rs.X ... .
  Bear case Rs.X ... ." Value treasury/net cash separately where material
  ("strip out treasury and the operating business trades at...").

## 12. Corporate Governance Checks

Three-column table (Parameter | Status | Comment) covering at minimum:
promoter holding, promoter pledge, independent directors, investor concalls,
related party transactions, auditor, SEBI/exchange penalties, dividend track
record, treasury/capital deployment, **shareholding & liquidity** (FII/DII/public
split, free float, average traded value / delivery volumes), plus any
company-specific items that came up in research.

Close with an honest paragraph separating red flags from structural quirks.

## 13. Triggers to Watch

5-6 bullets. Each trigger is dated, specific, and checkable ("Q1FY27 Hightech
revenue: even modest recovery from FY26's quarterly run-rate of Rs.40-41cr
confirms..."), never vague ("execution", "growth momentum"). State what the
trigger would confirm or deny.

## 14. Key Risks

5-6 bullets, most damaging first. Each: the mechanism of loss and what would
confirm the risk is materialising. Include the *analytical* risks - what cannot
be verified from public documents - not just business risks. Long-dated structural
risks (e.g. CBDC) go last with their horizon stated.

## 15. Management Team

Table: Name & Role | Background | Assessment. The assessment column is yours -
key-man risk, governance gaps, unproven successors, positives too. Close with a
verdict paragraph on the promoter/founder structure and which investor types it
suits.

## 16. Final Outlook

Restate `Rating | Base Target Price | Upside (CMP, Mcap)` on its own line, then
3-4 paragraphs: the structural transformation story; the valuation anchor and the
minimal assumptions it requires; why risk-reward is asymmetric (2-3 structural
facts); the risks that cap position sizing.

Footer on the last page:
"Strictly Confidential - for internal circulation only. This note is for
informational purposes and does not constitute investment advice."

# Pitch deck mapping

Build the .pptx from the finished note - same facts, no new claims. One idea per
slide, max ~6 bullets or one table per slide:

1. Title slide - company, tagline, the full rating line, date
2. Company overview (the 3-4 bullets, tightened)
3. Business cycles - one visual timeline/table row per cycle + one-line lessons
4. Segments - segment table + one takeaway per segment
5. Business model & moat - value chain in one diagram-style list, moat layers
6. Investment thesis - the 3-4 pillar headlines with one supporting stat each
7. Quarterly trend + annual financials - the two tables (split into two slides
   if cramped)
8. Balance sheet / treasury
9. Stock price story - phases with price ranges
10. Valuation - scenario table + base/bull/bear line
11. Governance & management - condensed checklist + key assessments
12. Triggers & risks - two columns
13. Final outlook + disclaimer

Keep tables to at most 6 columns on slides - drop the oldest years first.
